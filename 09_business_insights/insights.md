## Executive Summary

This analysis examines employee attrition patterns using the IBM HR dataset to identify key factors influencing employee turnover. By combining SQL analysis, Python-based exploratory data analysis, and advanced feature evaluation, we uncover critical trends related to job roles, compensation, work-life balance, and career progression.

The findings indicate that attrition is not random but strongly associated with specific organizational and employee-related factors. These insights provide a strong foundation for data-driven HR strategies aimed at improving employee retention.

## Overall Attrition Overview

Employee attrition represents a significant challenge for the organization. The dataset shows a measurable proportion of employees leaving the company, indicating potential gaps in engagement, compensation alignment, workload management, or growth opportunities.

Understanding the characteristics of employees who leave versus those who stay is essential to design targeted retention strategies rather than generic HR policies.

## Department-Level Attrition Insights

Attrition rates vary notably across departments, suggesting that employee turnover is influenced by department-specific work environments and management practices.

Departments with higher attrition tend to experience:
- Higher workload pressure
- Limited role mobility
- Increased overtime dependency

This variation highlights the importance of department-level interventions instead of organization-wide assumptions.

## Job Role and Experience Analysis

Certain job roles exhibit consistently higher attrition rates, particularly among early-career and mid-level employees. Employees with fewer total working years or limited tenure within the company are more likely to leave.

This trend indicates that the initial years of employment are critical for employee engagement, skill development, and expectation management.

## Compensation and Income Insights

Income-related variables show a strong relationship with employee attrition. Employees in lower income brackets demonstrate a higher likelihood of leaving, especially when compensation growth does not align with experience or job responsibility.

However, compensation alone is not the sole driver. Employees with competitive pay but limited career progression or high workload also show increased attrition risk.

## Work-Life Balance and Overtime Impact

Overtime emerges as one of the most influential factors contributing to employee attrition. Employees required to work overtime consistently show a significantly higher tendency to leave the organization.

This suggests that sustained workload pressure and poor work-life balance can erode employee satisfaction, even when other factors such as compensation are reasonable.

## Career Growth and Promotion Factors

Employees who have spent extended periods without promotion exhibit higher attrition rates. Limited career advancement opportunities appear to reduce long-term engagement and motivation.

The data suggests that employees value visible career progression and skill growth as much as financial incentives.

## Key Takeaways from the Analysis

- Attrition is influenced by a combination of compensation, workload, and career development factors.
- Overtime and work-life imbalance are among the strongest contributors to employee turnover.
- Early-career employees and specific job roles face higher attrition risks.
- Career stagnation significantly increases the likelihood of employees leaving the organization.
- Effective retention strategies must be targeted, role-specific, and data-driven.
