# 📊 Employee Attrition Analysis – Final Report

**Dataset:** IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance

---

## 1. Executive Summary

### Purpose

This section provides a concise, leadership-level overview of the analysis, key findings, and business implications.

### Summary

Employee attrition poses a significant challenge to organizational stability, productivity, and long-term talent retention. This project analyzes historical HR data from **1,470 employees** to identify the key drivers influencing employee attrition.

Using **Python, SQL, and data visualization techniques**, multiple workforce dimensions—including department, job role, compensation, overtime, and employee satisfaction—were examined to uncover meaningful patterns.

The analysis reveals that **overtime work, lower compensation levels, early-career tenure, and specific job roles** contribute disproportionately to employee attrition. These insights provide a strong foundation for **data-driven HR strategies** and establish readiness for future **predictive modeling initiatives**.

---

## 2. Business Problem Statement

High employee attrition results in increased recruitment and training costs, loss of organizational knowledge, reduced productivity, and lower employee morale. Reactive approaches to attrition management often fail to address underlying causes.

The objective of this analysis is to **identify patterns and key factors associated with employee exits** and translate them into actionable insights that help organizations **proactively reduce attrition and improve employee retention**.

## 5. Key Analytical Findings

### 5.1 Attrition by Department

* **Sales** and **Human Resources** exhibit the highest attrition rates.
* **Research & Development** shows comparatively stronger employee stability.

### 5.2 Attrition by Job Role

* Higher attrition observed in:

  * Laboratory Technicians
  * Sales Executives
  * Sales Representatives
* Lower attrition in managerial and senior research roles, indicating greater role stability.

### 5.3 Overtime Impact

* Employees working overtime are **significantly more likely to leave** the organization.
* This highlights **work-life imbalance** as a major contributor to attrition.

### 5.4 Compensation Influence

* Employees who left the organization earned **substantially lower average monthly income**.
* Compensation inequity is strongly associated with increased attrition risk.

## 7. Business Insights

Key insights derived from the analysis include:

* Excessive overtime contributes to employee burnout and exits
* Early-career employees require stronger engagement and support structures
* Sales roles need workload, incentive, and performance expectation realignment
* Competitive and equitable compensation improves employee retention

---

## 8. HR Recommendations

Based on the insights, the following high-impact HR actions are recommended:

* Reduce excessive and prolonged overtime practices
* Improve compensation benchmarking and pay equity
* Strengthen onboarding, mentoring, and early-career support programs
* Provide clear career progression and promotion pathways
* Continuously monitor attrition risk indicators using data analytics
